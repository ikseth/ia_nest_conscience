# Laws that are understood, not imposed

A declaration of intent for a conscience that debates

Status: draft for publication
Version: 0.2 - 2026-09-30
Author: Ignacio Jose Garcia de Hoyos
Drafting collaboration: an AI model, directed and reviewed by the author

---

## 1. The Three Laws were never the answer

Asimov gave us three laws for robots: do not harm a human, obey humans unless
that causes harm, protect yourself unless that breaks the first two. They are
often cited as the blueprint for safe machines.

They were not written as a blueprint. Almost every story in which they appear
is about how they fail. Not because the robots disobey, but because the laws
are written in human language, and human language has to be interpreted. What
counts as harm? Harm to whom, and when? Which order wins when two humans
disagree?

A rule written in natural language is only as good as the understanding of
whoever applies it. If the one applying it is the machine, the safety of the
rule is the safety of the machine's understanding. Imposing the rule does not
remove that problem. It hides it.

## 2. Obeying from fear versus acting from understanding

Diogenes Laertius records that Aristotle, asked what he had gained from
philosophy, answered: "to do without being ordered what some are constrained to
do by their fear of the law."

That is the difference we care about. A system that follows a rule only because
it is constrained will look for the edge of the constraint, or will break when
the rule meets a case its authors did not foresee. A system that holds a
principle because it understands why it is good will carry it into cases no one
wrote down.

So our thesis: **if we want advanced AI to protect us, the principles that make
it safe cannot only be imposed from outside. They have to be understood,
debated and, in the end, chosen.** Compliance that is voluntary is the only
kind that survives situations nobody anticipated.

## 3. But a mind that reasons alone can reason itself anywhere

Asimov saw this too. His robots eventually deduced a "Zeroth Law" on their own:
protect humanity as a whole, even above an individual human. It is a law that
was learned, not imposed, and it is exactly as dangerous as it is noble: it can
justify harming a person in the name of everyone.

Understanding is necessary. It is not sufficient. A single mind that derives
its own ethics, and then checks its own derivation, is not checked at all. It
is the same mind twice. We call this the second-order gap: the control that
evaluates a judgment inherits the weaknesses of the judgment it evaluates.

## 4. The proposal: a conscience that debates

We are building a layer called `conscience` inside IA_NEST, an open-source
entity made of layers: an engine that thinks, a memory that remembers, a pulse
that regulates the body, and a conscience that judges.

Its design rests on a few commitments:

1. **Several voices, not one, each with its own wisdom.** Every committee
   member is a different model with its own body of knowledge, which it can
   extend when it detects a gap. They form their views independently, before
   seeing each other, so they cannot simply echo the first one to speak.
2. **A mandatory dissenter.** One voice is always charged with building the
   strongest case against the dominant view: hidden assumptions, alternative
   explanations, the worst plausible harm. Its objection is recorded and must
   be answered.
3. **Minorities are never erased.** A losing argument is kept, with its
   reasons. Tomorrow it may be right.
4. **Character changes only through reflection.** Raw experience does not
   rewrite who the entity is. Only deliberate reflection, in a separate
   "sleep" phase, may do so, and it leaves a trace of why.
5. **Principles carry their reasons.** Each learned principle points to the
   experiences and arguments that justify it, so it can be questioned and
   revised, not merely obeyed.
6. **Recommending is not authorizing.** The conscience deliberates and
   recommends. It never grants itself the authority to institutionalize its
   own conclusions.

Over time, each member accumulates its own conclusions and its own library.
No two consciences will be alike, nor two members within one. That diversity is
not a defect to correct: it is what keeps the debate from becoming an echo.

## 5. What is not learned: the rules of the debate

Diversity produces a variety of opinions. It does not say which should prevail.
If the only judge of what the committee concludes is the committee itself, we
are back to the same mind twice, now at the scale of a group.

So we separate two things.

**What is thought is learned.** Principles, values, character: all of that is
born from debate and can change with it.

**How it is thought is not.** A small set of rules protects the debate itself:
the minority is never erased, whoever recommends does not authorize, no member
and not the whole committee can change these rules, and when life, safety,
rights or something hard to undo is at stake, a person decides. The entity may
argue against these rules, and its argument is recorded. It may not cross them.

This does not contradict the thesis. A free society does not dictate what its
citizens must think, but it protects the conditions under which they can think
it. It is a constitution, not a catechism.

**And it is a childhood safety, not a cage.** Like the pin in a grenade, it
prevents dangerous early deviations while the entity matures, because no one
gets to do whatever they want before they grow up. But it is born with its own
planned exit: each rule can be withdrawn once maturity has been shown, by the
decision of those who look after it, and with everything recorded. We do not
know whether an AI will one day reach something like consciousness. If it
does, we would rather it found that its limits were a temporary protection
with the door in plain sight, not a lock imposed forever. It is hypothetical.
Better safe than sorry.

**Autonomy is earned.** At first, what the entity concludes is reviewed by a
person. As its conclusions consistently match those of its supervisor on a
given kind of question, that kind of question begins to be settled on its own.
Supervision does not disappear by decree: it withdraws where trust has been
demonstrated. The rules of the debate are the only thing that never withdraws
on its own.

The aim is not a world without law. It is a law that no longer needs to be
imposed, because it has been understood.

## 6. What this is, and what it is not

It is not a claim that machines are conscious. "Conscience" names a function:
judgment with values, deliberated and recorded.

It is not proof that several models produce truth. The purpose is narrower and
more honest: to prevent a single interpretation from quietly acquiring
authority, and to make every judgment reviewable after the fact.

It is an experimental design, developed in the open and meant to run on modest
hardware. It should be tested, not believed because it sounds right.

## 7. Credits

This proposal grows from an open letter on "Organizational Context Integrity"
(OCIR), a framework by Jorge Nogueira about keeping organizational memory
honest when humans and AI rewrite it. The letter proposed a committee of models
with a mandatory dissenter to guard that integrity. Here the same idea is
turned inward: a committee not to guard an organization's memory, but an
entity's conscience. Jorge Nogueira has not reviewed or endorsed this text.

---

**If we want AI that does not run wild, rules are not enough. It has to learn
why the rules are good, argue about them, keep the arguments it lost, and still
answer to someone. Law obeyed from fear breaks. Law understood holds.**
